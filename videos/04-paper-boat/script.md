# Script — "The Paper Boat" (animated short story, about 5:00)

**Format:** narrated animated short film for a faceless channel
**Style:** warm 2D storybook animation, soft rain, cozy colors; one gentle narrator voice (suggested: Celine)
**Length:** about 5:00. About 420 narrated words (about 3.5 min at a slow storytelling pace of about 120 wpm); the remaining time is music-only visual beats, like the reference film's wordless moments.
**Characters:** **Mira** (7, yellow raincoat, two puff buns) · **Grandpa** (white hair, round glasses, green cardigan) · **Mom** (voice only, off-screen)

---

### SCENE 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:22)
**[VISUAL: Rain on a window. Grandpa sits at the kitchen table holding a square of white paper. His hands don't move.]**

NARRATOR:
Every time it rained, Grandpa made a paper boat. He had done it for sixty years.
But this morning, he sat at the table, holding the paper… and he couldn't remember how.

---

### SCENE 2 — THE RAINY-DAY RULE (0:22–1:00)
**[VISUAL: Warm flashback colors. Younger-looking Grandpa folding quickly; little Mira laughing; two boats racing down the street gutter.]**

When Mira was little, rainy days were the best days. Grandpa would fold a boat in eleven quick moves: fold, fold, flip, pull. Then a boat appeared, like magic.
They raced them down the stream along the street, shouting and splashing.
And Grandpa had one rule. "Before a boat sails," he said, "you write a wish inside. The rain carries it away."

**[MUSIC SWELL, 3 s]**

---

### SCENE 3 — GRANDPA STARTS TO FORGET (1:00–1:40)
**[VISUAL: Present day. Grandpa puts his keys in the fridge. He calls Mira by her mother's name. Mira's smile fades.]**

But lately, Grandpa had been forgetting things. Small things at first: his keys turned up in the fridge. Then bigger things. Sometimes he called Mira by her mother's name.
"Grandpa's memory is getting tired," Mom said softly. "Some days will be harder than others."
Mira looked at the rain. Then at the paper in Grandpa's hands.
If Grandpa couldn't remember how to make the boat, she would learn it… and teach him back.

---

### SCENE 4 — THE FAILURES (1:40–2:40)
**[VISUAL: Mira at the table, tongue out, concentrating. Each failed boat pops up with a little sound effect.]**

Her first boat looked like a hat.
**[VISUAL: Mira wears it. Beat.]**

Her second looked like a frog.
**[VISUAL: Paper frog flops over.]**

Her third… was just a ball.
**[VISUAL: Crumpled ball bounces off her head.]**

She tried one in the sink. It sank in two seconds.
**[VISUAL: Sad bubbles.]**

And outside, the rain was getting lighter. Soon, it would stop. And the stream along the street would be gone.

---

### SCENE 5 — THE TIN BOX (2:40–3:20)
**[VISUAL: Mira searches Grandpa's shelf. She finds an old blue tin box. Inside: dozens of little paper boats.]**

On Grandpa's shelf, behind the books, Mira found an old blue tin box.
Inside were paper boats. Dozens of them. Every boat Grandpa had ever kept.
Mira had an idea. Very, very carefully, she unfolded one… one fold at a time. And the creases in the paper showed her every step.
In the middle of the paper, there was a wish, written in Grandpa's handwriting.
**[ON SCREEN: handwritten "I wish Mira always has someone to sail boats with."]**

Mira read it twice.

---

### SCENE 6 — THE PERFECT BOAT (3:20–3:50)
**[VISUAL: Mira refolding along the old creases. Fold, fold, flip, pull. A perfect boat.]**

She folded it back, following Grandpa's old lines. Fold. Fold. Flip. Pull.
A perfect boat.
She ran to the kitchen.

---

### SCENE 7 — HANDS THAT REMEMBER (3:50–4:30)
**[VISUAL: Close-up. Mira's small hands on top of Grandpa's big hands, guiding the paper.]**

Grandpa looked at the boat like he had never seen one before.
So Mira took a new piece of paper, put it in his hands, and placed her hands on top of his.
"Fold," she whispered. "Fold. Flip…"
And then something happened. Grandpa's fingers began to move on their own. Faster. Surer. He made the last fold all by himself.
He looked up and smiled. "Mira," he said. "You write the wish."

---

### SCENE 8 — THE WISH (4:30–5:00)
**[VISUAL: Outside, the last trickle of rain. Two boats sail side by side down the street stream. Mira and Grandpa hold hands.]**

They made it just in time. The last stream of rainwater carried both boats down the street, side by side.
Mira never told anyone what she wrote inside.
**[ON SCREEN: handwritten "I wish Grandpa remembers today."]**

Grandpa didn't remember everything after that. But every time it rained… his hands did.

**[END CARD: "The Paper Boat" + "Call someone you love today."]**

---

## Production notes
- **Pace:** slow and warm. Leave 1–2 s pauses after emotional lines. The music carries the gaps.
- **Music:** soft piano plus light strings. Use rain ambience throughout and cut it at "A perfect boat."
- **Sensitivity:** memory loss is shown gently. No diagnosis is named and it's never played for laughs (the comedy beats are only Mira's failed boats and the keys).
- **Originality:** this is an original story. It borrows only the general *structure* of the reference (small task → funny failures → guidance from the past → teamwork → reveal), not its characters, plot, setting or visuals.
