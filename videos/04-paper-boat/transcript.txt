Every time it rained, Grandpa made a paper boat. He had done it for sixty years.
But this morning, he sat at the table, holding the paper… and he couldn't remember how.

When Mira was little, rainy days were the best days. Grandpa would fold a boat in eleven quick moves: fold, fold, flip, pull. Then a boat appeared, like magic.
They raced them down the stream along the street, shouting and splashing.
And Grandpa had one rule. "Before a boat sails," he said, "you write a wish inside. The rain carries it away."

But lately, Grandpa had been forgetting things. Small things at first: his keys turned up in the fridge. Then bigger things. Sometimes he called Mira by her mother's name.
"Grandpa's memory is getting tired," Mom said softly. "Some days will be harder than others."
Mira looked at the rain. Then at the paper in Grandpa's hands.
If Grandpa couldn't remember how to make the boat, she would learn it… and teach him back.

Her first boat looked like a hat.
Her second looked like a frog.
Her third… was just a ball.
She tried one in the sink. It sank in two seconds.
And outside, the rain was getting lighter. Soon, it would stop. And the stream along the street would be gone.

On Grandpa's shelf, behind the books, Mira found an old blue tin box.
Inside were paper boats. Dozens of them. Every boat Grandpa had ever kept.
Mira had an idea. Very, very carefully, she unfolded one… one fold at a time. And the creases in the paper showed her every step.
In the middle of the paper, there was a wish, written in Grandpa's handwriting.
Mira read it twice.

She folded it back, following Grandpa's old lines. Fold. Fold. Flip. Pull.
A perfect boat.
She ran to the kitchen.

Grandpa looked at the boat like he had never seen one before.
So Mira took a new piece of paper, put it in his hands, and placed her hands on top of his.
"Fold," she whispered. "Fold. Flip…"
And then something happened. Grandpa's fingers began to move on their own. Faster. Surer. He made the last fold all by himself.
He looked up and smiled. "Mira," he said. "You write the wish."

They made it just in time. The last stream of rainwater carried both boats down the street, side by side.
Mira never told anyone what she wrote inside.
Grandpa didn't remember everything after that. But every time it rained… his hands did.
