# Reference Analysis — "Hair Love" (Sony Pictures Animation)

## 1. The channel
- **@SonyAnimation** is a studio channel: trailers, clips, featurettes and a few full short films.
- **Hair Love** is one of the studio's most-watched uploads. It's a full short film that keeps getting found through search and recommendations years after release, thanks to the Oscar win and lots of sharing.
- **Lesson for a faceless channel:** studio trailers don't carry over, but the *short-film format* does. Emotional, complete, family-centered stories are evergreen: people share them, rewatch them and send them to family.

## 2. What the video does (story pattern)
| Beat | What happens in Hair Love | The reusable pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Small everyday problem** | A dad tries to style his daughter's hair for the first time | A tiny, relatable task with a clear goal |
| **Comedy of failure** | The hair "fights back" in visual gags | 2–3 escalating funny failures keep it light |
| **A guide from someone absent** | A tutorial video recorded by the mom helps them | A message or object from someone who isn't there |
| **Teamwork** | Dad and daughter finally do it together | Success through connection, not skill |
| **The reveal** | The real reason it mattered is shown at the end | A late twist that changes what the whole story meant |
| **Warm ending** | The family together | A quiet final image with hope |

## 3. Hooks and retention
- **Almost no dialogue.** The story is told through faces, music and visual comedy, so it works for any language and any age.
- **The question in the first 20 seconds:** "Can they do it?" A simple goal the viewer roots for.
- **Hidden stakes:** viewers sense something is off (why is mom only on a screen?), which keeps them watching for the answer.
- **The emotional payoff comes last.** People rewatch the beginning after the reveal, which boosts watch time.
- **Short:** about 7 minutes, with no filler.

## 4. How we adapt it (original story, not a copy)
Faceless story channels perform best with **a gentle narrator, a two-character family story, a small physical object at the center, and a late emotional reveal**.

Our story: **"The Paper Boat"**. A girl whose grandpa is losing his memory learns to fold his paper boats by carefully unfolding his old ones, then helps his hands remember.
- Different characters, setting, object and twist from Hair Love. It shares only the *structure*: small task, funny failures, guidance from the past, teamwork, reveal.
- **Topic strength:** grandparent stories and memory loss are huge evergreen emotional niches, with strong sharing, comments ("this made me call my grandpa") and rewatches.
